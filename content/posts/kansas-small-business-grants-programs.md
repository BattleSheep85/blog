One of the things that surprised me about Wichita's startup scene is how much funding and support is actually available if you know where to look. Most business owners I talk to have no idea these programs exist, which means less competition for you. Kansas isn't throwing money around wildly, but there are legitimate grant programs, matching funds, and free resources that can help you get a new business off the ground or grow an existing one.

Here's a rundown of the programs worth knowing about, with real numbers and actual application details.

<!--more-->

## Kansas R&D Acceleration Grants

This is one of the best-kept secrets for Kansas tech and manufacturing startups. The Kansas Department of Commerce offers R&D Acceleration Grants that provide a 50/50 match up to $25,000 for research and development expenses.

**How it works:** You spend money on qualified R&D activities (prototyping, testing, product development, feasibility studies), and Kansas matches your spending dollar for dollar up to $25K. So you put in $25K, the state puts in $25K, and you've got $50K of R&D budget for the cost of $25K.

**Who qualifies:** Kansas-based companies working on technology development, product innovation, or applied research. The program favors businesses that are developing something new, not just running their existing operation.

**What to know:** The application process is competitive but not impossibly bureaucratic. You'll need a solid project description, budget breakdown, and explanation of expected outcomes. Applications typically open in cycles, so check the Kansas Department of Commerce website for current deadlines.

This is real money for a small startup that needs to build a prototype or test a concept. If you're in hardware, software, biotech, or advanced manufacturing, look at this first.

## Kansas FAST Program

If you're thinking about applying for federal SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research) or STTR (Small Business Technology Transfer) grants, the Kansas FAST program helps you get your proposal ready.

**What it provides:** Up to $3,000 per company for SBIR/STTR proposal development. This covers things like proposal writing assistance, budget development, and application review.

**Why it matters:** SBIR/STTR grants are federal programs that provide non-dilutive funding (meaning you don't give up equity) for R&D. Phase I awards are typically $150,000 to $275,000. Phase II can go up to $1 million or more. But the applications are competitive and the proposals need to be strong. FAST funding helps you put together a professional submission instead of winging it.

**The math:** You use $3K from FAST to write a strong SBIR proposal. If you win a Phase I award, that's $150K+ in non-dilutive funding. Even if your odds of winning are 20%, the expected value is excellent.

## ACCEL-KS at Groover Labs

If you haven't been to Groover Labs in Old Town, you should go. It's a makerspace and startup accelerator at 334 N. Commerce in Wichita, and it's one of the best resources in the city for hardware startups and tech companies.

**ACCEL-KS** (Advancing Commercialization and Collaboration to Leverage Entrepreneurism in Kansas) is a program run through Groover Labs that provides:

- **Mentoring** from experienced entrepreneurs and industry professionals
- **Grant funding** for prototyping and product development
- **Access to equipment** including 3D printers, electronics workbenches, CNC machines, and other prototyping tools
- **Connections** to the Wichita business and investment community

The program is designed for startups that are past the idea stage and working on building something real. If you've got a concept and need help turning it into a product, this is the place.

Groover Labs also hosts regular events, workshops, and networking opportunities. Even if you don't join ACCEL-KS, getting plugged into the Groover Labs community is worth your time.

## Wichita Business Loan Program

The City of Wichita maintains a business loan program with a $9 million pool designed to help small businesses that might not qualify for traditional bank financing.

**Key details:**

- Loans for startups and existing small businesses within Wichita city limits
- Funds can be used for equipment, working capital, leasehold improvements, and other business purposes
- More flexible terms than conventional bank loans
- Designed to fill the gap for businesses that are viable but don't check every box a bank requires

This isn't grant money (you have to pay it back), but the terms are typically more favorable than what you'd get from a commercial lender, especially if you're early-stage or don't have extensive collateral.

Contact the City of Wichita Economic Development office for current availability and application requirements.

## Kansas SBDC at Wichita State University

The Kansas Small Business Development Center at WSU provides free, confidential advising for small business owners and aspiring entrepreneurs. Free. As in $0.

**What they offer:**

- **One-on-one business advising.** An experienced advisor helps you work through business planning, financial projections, marketing strategy, operational challenges, and growth planning. This isn't a one-time meeting. They'll work with you over months or years.
- **Workshops and training.** Regular sessions on topics like business plan development, financial management, marketing, and access to capital.
- **Market research.** The SBDC has access to market research databases and tools that would cost thousands to access on your own.
- **Loan package preparation.** If you're applying for an SBA loan, bank financing, or the Wichita Business Loan Program, the SBDC will help you prepare a professional loan package.

I can't overstate how valuable this is. You're getting expert business advice backed by WSU and the SBA, and it doesn't cost you a dime. If you're starting a business in Wichita and you haven't connected with the SBDC, you're leaving free help on the table.

They're located at the WSU campus, and you can schedule an appointment through the Kansas SBDC website.

## SBA disaster loans (when you need them)

This isn't a startup program, but it's worth knowing about. When Kansas gets a federal disaster declaration (which happens regularly during tornado season), the SBA offers disaster loans to affected businesses:

- **Up to $2 million** per business
- **4% interest rate** (approximate, varies by declaration)
- **Up to 30-year terms**
- Covers physical damage, economic injury, and business recovery

You don't want to need this, but if a tornado, severe storm, or flood damages your business, this is often the most affordable capital available for recovery.

## How to stack these programs

Here's the strategy I'd recommend for a Wichita startup:

1. **Start with the SBDC.** Get free advising, develop your business plan, and identify which funding sources fit your situation. This costs nothing and gives you a foundation.

2. **Apply for R&D Acceleration Grants** if you're doing product development. $25K in matching funds goes a long way for a startup.

3. **Use FAST funding** to develop an SBIR/STTR proposal if your technology has federal applications (defense, healthcare, energy, etc.).

4. **Plug into Groover Labs** for prototyping resources, mentoring, and community. Apply for ACCEL-KS if you're at the right stage.

5. **Explore the Wichita Business Loan Program** for capital needs that grants don't cover.

These programs aren't mutually exclusive. You can use SBDC advising while applying for R&D grants while building your prototype at Groover Labs. Stack every advantage you can.
