I audit M365 licensing for small businesses regularly, and the same problem comes up every time: everyone is on the same plan, and it's usually the wrong one. Either the whole company is on Business Premium at $22/user/month when half the staff only checks email on their phone, or everyone is on Basic and three people had to buy standalone Office licenses because they need Excel.

Microsoft lets you mix and match licenses. You should be doing exactly that.

<!--more-->

## The four plans, simplified

Here's what you're actually choosing between (prices as of early 2026):

**Microsoft 365 Business Basic: $6/user/month**
- Web and mobile versions of Office apps (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook)
- Exchange Online email (50GB mailbox)
- OneDrive (1TB storage)
- SharePoint Online
- Microsoft Teams
- No desktop Office apps

**Microsoft 365 Business Standard: $12.50/user/month**
- Everything in Basic, plus desktop versions of Office apps
- This is the most common plan, and the right one for most knowledge workers

**Microsoft 365 Business Premium: $22/user/month**
- Everything in Standard, plus Intune (device management), Defender for Office 365, Entra ID P1 (Conditional Access), Azure Information Protection
- This is the security and compliance tier

**Microsoft 365 Apps for Business: $12.50/user/month**
- Desktop Office apps, OneDrive, SharePoint
- No Exchange email, no Teams
- Rarely the right choice, but useful in specific scenarios

## The July 2026 price increase

Microsoft announced price increases effective July 2026:

- Business Basic goes from $6 to $7/user/month
- Business Standard goes from $12.50 to $14/user/month
- Business Premium goes from $22 to $24.75/user/month (some sources say $24.50)

For a 25-person company all on Standard, that's an extra $37.50/month, or $450/year. Not catastrophic, but it adds up, and it's one more reason to right-size your licenses now.

## How to mix licenses and save money

Here's the approach I use with every client:

### Step 1: Categorize your users

Look at your staff and figure out who actually needs what:

- **Email and Teams only (Basic):** Warehouse workers, field staff, part-time employees, anyone who doesn't need desktop Office apps. They can use the web versions of Word and Excel, which are honestly pretty good now.
- **Office power users (Standard):** Accountants, admin staff, salespeople, anyone who lives in Excel, Word, or PowerPoint and needs the desktop apps.
- **Security-critical roles (Premium):** Executives, IT admins, anyone with access to sensitive data, remote workers on personal devices (BYOD). The Conditional Access and Intune features in Premium are what you're paying for here.

### Step 2: Do the math

Let's say you have a 25-person company:

**Everyone on Standard (the lazy approach):**
25 users x $12.50 = $312.50/month ($3,750/year)

**Mixed licensing:**
- 8 warehouse/field staff on Basic: 8 x $6 = $48/month
- 12 office workers on Standard: 12 x $12.50 = $150/month
- 5 executives/admins on Premium: 5 x $22 = $110/month
- Total: $308/month ($3,696/year)

In this example the savings are modest because Premium costs offset the Basic savings. But here's a more common scenario I see:

**Everyone on Premium (the "our IT guy said we need it" approach):**
25 users x $22 = $550/month ($6,600/year)

**Mixed licensing (same split as above):**
$308/month ($3,696/year)

That's $2,904/year back in your pocket. And the warehouse staff who only check email on their phone don't notice any difference.

### Step 3: Reassess quarterly

People change roles. New hires come in. Someone who was on Basic gets promoted to a position that needs desktop apps. Check your licensing every quarter to make sure it still matches reality.

## Common licensing mistakes

**Buying Premium for everyone "just in case."** The Intune and Conditional Access features in Premium are genuinely useful, but only if you're actually going to configure and use them. Paying $22/user for Premium and then never setting up a single Conditional Access policy is burning money.

**Forgetting about shared mailboxes.** Shared mailboxes (info@, sales@, support@) are free up to 50GB and don't require a license. Stop assigning licenses to them.

**Not using the license usage reports.** M365 admin center has built-in reports that show you which users are actually using which apps. If someone on Standard hasn't opened a desktop Office app in 90 days, they probably belong on Basic.

**Ignoring the annual commitment discount.** Annual commitment pricing is typically 15-20% cheaper than month-to-month. If your headcount is stable, commit annually and save.

## What about the new Copilot add-on?

Microsoft 365 Copilot is an additional $30/user/month on top of your base license. That's not a typo. For a 25-person company, that's $750/month or $9,000/year. My honest take: wait. The AI features are impressive in demos but inconsistent in real-world use. Let it mature for another year before committing budget to it.
