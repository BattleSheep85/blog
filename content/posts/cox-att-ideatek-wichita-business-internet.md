Picking an ISP for your Wichita business shouldn't be this hard, but it is. Cox has the biggest footprint, AT&T has fiber in some areas, and IdeaTek is the scrappy Kansas-based provider that keeps winning fans. There are actually 23 ISP options in the Wichita market, but for most businesses it comes down to these three. So let's break down what you're actually getting with each one.

I've set up and managed business internet connections across Wichita for years, and I have strong opinions on this. Fair warning.

<!--more-->

## Cox Business

Cox is the default choice for most Wichita businesses because they're everywhere. Almost every commercial address in the metro can get Cox service. That's the good news. Here's the rest.

**Speeds:** Cox Business offers plans from 25 Mbps up to 10 Gbps on their fiber product. Most small businesses end up on a 100/20 or 250/30 coax plan. Upload speeds on cable plans are the weak point. If you're pushing data to the cloud, doing video calls, or running any kind of hosted service, those upload numbers matter more than download.

**Reliability:** This is where Cox gets rough. Customer satisfaction sits around 3.17 out of 5, and that matches what I've seen in the field. Businesses report intermittent drops every 20 to 30 minutes, and there have been multi-day outages (some lasting 5 days or more). When there's an outage, good luck getting through to business support in a reasonable time.

**Cost:** Cox Business internet starts around $80/month for basic plans. Fiber is more expensive but you get symmetrical speeds. Watch the contract terms, because promotional rates expire and your bill can jump significantly.

**The verdict:** Cox works fine when it works. But when it doesn't, you're stuck waiting, and for a business that depends on internet connectivity, "mostly reliable" isn't good enough.

## AT&T Business

AT&T has been expanding fiber in Wichita, but coverage is still uneven. Some business parks have it, some don't, and there's no great way to know until you check your specific address.

**Speeds:** AT&T Fiber business plans go up to 5 Gbps symmetrical in areas where fiber is available. If you can only get DSL at your location, walk away. AT&T DSL is not a business-grade product in 2026.

**Reliability:** Where AT&T has fiber infrastructure, it's generally solid. Fiber is inherently more reliable than cable because it's less susceptible to interference, weather issues, and neighborhood congestion. But AT&T's customer service and installation process can be painful. Long lead times for business installs and a lot of back-and-forth on provisioning.

**Cost:** Competitive with Cox on fiber plans. AT&T often runs aggressive promotions for new business customers, but again, read the contract carefully.

**The verdict:** If AT&T Fiber is available at your address, it's worth quoting. If all they can offer is DSL or fixed wireless, keep looking.

## IdeaTek

IdeaTek is the one I get the most questions about, and for good reason. They're a Kansas-based fiber provider headquartered in Buhler, and they've been expanding aggressively into the Wichita market.

**Speeds:** Plans go up to 8 Gbps symmetrical. Even their entry-level business plans offer symmetrical upload and download, which is a huge advantage over Cox's cable offerings. For a business doing cloud backups, video conferencing, or running SaaS applications, symmetrical speeds make a real difference.

**Reliability:** Customer satisfaction is 4.75 out of 5, which is outstanding for an ISP. They're building their own fiber network from scratch, which means modern infrastructure and fewer legacy problems. Their support is responsive and local. You're not calling a national call center in another time zone.

**Coverage:** This is the catch. IdeaTek's fiber currently passes about 19.9% of Wichita homes and businesses. They're building out fast, but if they're not in your area yet, you'll have to wait or look elsewhere.

**Cost:** Comparable to Cox and AT&T for similar speeds. Where IdeaTek wins is the value per dollar, because you get symmetrical speeds and dramatically better support.

**The verdict:** If IdeaTek is available at your location, it should be your first choice. Full stop.

## What I recommend for most Wichita businesses

Here's my order of preference:

1. **IdeaTek Fiber** if it's available at your address. Best speeds, best reliability, best support, local company.
2. **AT&T Fiber** as a solid second choice where available. Good speeds, good reliability, rougher customer experience.
3. **Cox Business Fiber** if you need the Cox footprint and can get their fiber product (not cable).
4. **Cox Business Cable** as a last resort. It'll work most of the time, but plan for outages and slow uploads.

## The real answer: redundancy

Here's what I tell every Wichita business owner who asks me about internet: no single ISP is good enough to bet your business on. If your revenue depends on being online, you need a backup connection.

The best setup for a small business is a primary fiber connection (IdeaTek or AT&T) with a cellular failover on a different carrier. A good dual-WAN router or firewall can switch between connections automatically when one goes down. Your employees might not even notice the failover happened.

A Cradlepoint or Peplink cellular gateway with a Verizon or T-Mobile business plan runs $50 to $150/month and gives you an independent backup path that doesn't share the same last-mile infrastructure as your primary ISP. That $100/month is cheap insurance against the $5,000/day you lose when the internet is down and nobody can process transactions, answer emails, or access cloud apps.
